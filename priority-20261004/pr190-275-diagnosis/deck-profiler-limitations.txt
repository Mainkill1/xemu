MemoryRegion / FlatView reference hypothesis:
Matching native DWARF resolves a few samples in flatview_ref, flatview_unref, address_space_get_flatview, and address_space_cache_init. Their footprint is small relative to resampler/HRTF and kernel scheduling activity in all three captures. Sparse sample counts cannot measure their small difference precisely. No cache misses, HITM, locked-memory latency, or cacheline-contention event was recorded; the captures neither establish shared-refcount contention nor rule out a small effect.

Clock/thermal observations:
Temperature rose with run order, but mean CPU clock did not fall monotonically (2526/2484/2509 MHz). There is no measured mode-linked clock reduction in these captures. One-second frequency readings cannot establish or exclude short power/thermal limits.

Interpretation and next boundary:
The sample callback is only one part of ongoing audio and emulation work; guest CPU, resampling/HRTF, and scheduling remain active. These observations support investigating the remaining costs, but do not establish why a small reader benefit fails to change broad game frame means. The next /proc-only profiles should separate actual worker CPU/runqueue behavior from hardware sampling overhead. Preserve the unequal audio-work caveat and avoid significance claims from one run per mode.
